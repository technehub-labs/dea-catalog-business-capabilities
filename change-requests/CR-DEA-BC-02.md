1. Purpose

Execute the evidence-based investigation required to establish the candidate universe from which the canonical DEA First-Order Business Capability Catalog will be constructed.

This CR does not authorize immediate publication of a final capability taxonomy.

Its purpose is to establish a defensible research corpus and systematically derive, compare, normalize and classify candidate Business Capabilities.

The outcome shall be:

Evidence Corpus
      ↓
Candidate Universe
      ↓
Candidate Classification
      ↓
Normalization
      ↓
First-Order Candidates
      ↓
Canonical Admission

---

2. Guiding Principle

The investigation shall answer:

What enduring abilities recur across materially different enterprises and are therefore suitable candidates for the first-order DEA Business Capability vocabulary?

It shall not answer:

What capabilities can we invent to fill the ECF?

The ECF remains an organizing framework rather than the source of capability candidates.

---

3. Current Repository Baseline

The target repository currently identifies Business Capability as:

an enduring ability an enterprise possesses or requires to produce, enable, control, preserve or realize a meaningful business outcome.

It identifies the catalog as the canonical first-order capability collection and explicitly distinguishes it from process taxonomies, ECF-cell filling exercises, organizational charts, system inventories and outcome lists. (GitHub⁠)

The repository currently remains a scaffold and explicitly gates capability population on CR-DEA-BC-01. (GitHub⁠)

This CR therefore begins the next authorized phase.

---

4. Research Question

The primary research question is:

Which Business Capabilities are sufficiently fundamental, durable, implementation-independent and enterprise-general to belong in the DEA first-order catalog?

Secondary questions:

1. Which abilities recur across materially different enterprise types?
2. Which apparent capabilities are actually processes?
3. Which are functions?
4. Which are organizational constructs?
5. Which are outcomes?
6. Which are services?
7. Which are resources?
8. Which are systems or technology capabilities?
9. Which candidates are industry-specific?
10. Which candidates are specializations of broader candidates?
11. Which candidates are synonyms or overlapping formulations?
12. Which capabilities have a stable business-object focus?
13. Which capabilities can be meaningfully related to the ECF?
14. Which ECF mappings are primary versus secondary?
15. Where does evidence remain insufficient?

---

5. Research Discipline

The investigation shall maintain a strict separation between:

Source Evidence
      ↓
Analytical Interpretation
      ↓
DEA Candidate
      ↓
DEA Decision

An external framework or source shall never become normative merely because it contains a particular capability name.

External material is evidence.

DEA’s governed semantic model remains authoritative.

---

6. Evidence Source Classes

The research corpus shall draw from several independent classes.

6.1 Business Architecture

Business architecture material is important because it explicitly distinguishes capabilities from processes, organization and implementation.

The Business Architecture Guild’s published material describes capabilities as business building blocks and emphasizes business-object focus when capabilities are decomposed. (businessarchitectureguild.org⁠)

This is useful evidence for:

* capability identity;
* decomposition;
* business-object focus;
* capability/value-stream distinction.

It shall not be copied as the DEA taxonomy.

---

6.2 Enterprise Architecture

Independent enterprise architecture material shall be used to test:

* durability;
* implementation independence;
* enterprise-wide applicability;
* relationships with processes, services, functions and systems.

For example, SAP’s published guidance describes Business Capabilities as business-perspective constructs that define what is required rather than how technical or organizational solutions are implemented, and distinguishes them from Business Processes. (SAP Help Portal⁠)

Again, this is corroborating evidence rather than DEA authority.

---

6.3 Cross-Industry Process Evidence

APQC’s Process Classification Framework shall be used as a process evidence source, not as a capability model.

APQC explicitly defines its PCF as a taxonomy of business processes and states that it organizes business work rather than capability semantics. (APQC⁠)

This distinction is especially valuable to DEA because recurring processes can reveal recurring underlying capabilities.

The current APQC PCF is version 8.0, with definitions and measures available for its process elements. (APQC⁠)

The investigation shall therefore use PCF evidence to ask:

What durable ability must exist for these recurring classes of work to be possible?

rather than:

Which APQC process names should become DEA capabilities?

---

7. Cross-Industry Versus Industry Evidence

APQC explicitly maintains both cross-industry and industry-specific PCFs. (APQC⁠)

This provides an important analytical separation:

Cross-Industry Evidence
        │
        ▼
Potential First-Order Capability

versus:

Industry-Specific Evidence
        │
        ▼
Potential Specialization

Industry-specific material shall therefore be retained because it helps establish:

* specialization boundaries;
* parent-child relationships;
* industry-specific business objects;
* semantic distinctions.

It shall not automatically enter the first-order catalog.

A theme such as:

does not automatically mean:

is a canonical capability.

Likewise:

People

does not automatically mean:

Human Capital Management

and:

Technology

does not automatically mean:

Technology Management

The research must establish:

* whether a durable ability exists;
* its semantic boundary;
* its outcome;
* its business-object focus;
* its relationship with other candidates;
* its enterprise-general status.

13. Important Finding: “Common” Is Not Sufficient

A capability candidate should not be admitted merely because many frameworks contain it.

We require two distinct forms of evidence:

Frequency Evidence
+
Semantic Validity

A concept may be common but still be:

* a process;
* a function;
* an organization;
* an implementation;
* a technology;
* an outcome;
* an overly broad grouping.

Therefore:

Commonality is evidence of recurrence, not proof of capability status.

---

14. Business-Object Focus

The investigation shall identify the principal business object in focus.

For example:

Customer Management
        │
        ▼
     Customer

A capability decomposition should retain that object focus.

The Business Architecture Guild’s published metamodel explicitly uses Customer Management to illustrate this principle: child capabilities remain concerned with the customer rather than drifting into agreements, products or financial accounts. (businessarchitectureguild.org⁠)

This is a useful constraint for DEA capability decomposition.

---

15. Candidate Abstraction Test

Each candidate shall be tested at the correct abstraction level.

Too broad:

Enterprise Management
Business Operations
Value Delivery

Potentially useful:

Financial Management
Customer Management
Workforce Management

Too granular:

Invoice Validation
Customer Email Handling
Employee Timesheet Processing

The objective is to identify a stable building block rather than an entire enterprise or a single activity.

Public business-architecture guidance similarly warns against capabilities that are too broad to be useful and emphasizes clear, unambiguous boundaries. (Deliver good DfE services⁠)

---

16. Universal Enterprise Test

A candidate shall be assessed against the following question:

Could an enterprise meaningfully lack this ability and still operate as the same kind of bounded value-exchanging enterprise?

This is not a simplistic yes/no requirement.

The investigation must distinguish:

Universally Necessary
Commonly Necessary
Contextually Necessary
Industry Necessary
Optional

The first-order catalog should prioritize universally necessary and strongly enterprise-general capabilities, while retaining commonly necessary candidates for further analysis.

---

17. Possession Versus Requirement

The investigation shall preserve the distinction:

Required
Possessed
Sourced
Realized

An enterprise may require an ability without directly performing the associated work.

For example, an enterprise may source:

* payroll;
* hosting;
* legal services;
* facilities management;
* logistics.

Outsourcing the realization does not necessarily eliminate the enterprise’s need for the underlying capability.

This principle is already reflected in the repository’s current guiding principles. (GitHub⁠)

---

18. Capability Versus Capacity

The previously investigated Capability:Ability:Capacity distinction remains applicable.

The catalog shall not collapse:

Capacity
Ability
Capability

into one term.

A Business Capability is the enterprise-level semantic construct.

Capacity concerns the amount, extent or available potential of realization.

Ability concerns what can be done.

Capability expresses the durable enterprise ability in the context of a business outcome.

This distinction shall be preserved in subsequent DEA semantic work.

23. Evidence Strength

Evidence shall be rated:

E0: No Evidence
E1: Single Source
E2: Multiple Independent Sources
E3: Cross-Enterprise Corroboration
E4: Cross-Industry Corroboration
E5: Strong Enterprise-General Evidence

The rating measures evidence, not semantic correctness.

A high evidence rating does not automatically create a canonical capability.

---

24. Confidence

Analytical confidence shall be tracked separately:

Low
Medium
High

This allows:

Strong Evidence + Low Semantic Confidence

to remain possible.

For example, a term may appear everywhere but have ambiguous semantic boundaries.

25. ECF Mapping Begins After Candidate Evaluation

The investigation shall not begin by assigning every candidate to an ECF cell.

Instead:

Candidate
   ↓
Capability Test
   ↓
Semantic Boundary
   ↓
ECF Mapping

The ECF mapping shall then ask:

1. Which Domain is the capability primarily concerned with?
2. At what Stage is it first initiated?
3. Does it legitimately participate elsewhere?
4. Does mapping expose an unexpected semantic conflict?
5. Does the mapping suggest that the capability is actually a specialization or composite?

---

26. ECF Mapping as Validation

ECF mapping shall therefore serve two purposes:

Organization

Provide a common coordinate.

Semantic Validation

Expose questionable capability boundaries.

For example, if a candidate appears to belong simultaneously and equally to unrelated ECF domains, the investigation should ask whether:

* the candidate is too broad;
* it is actually several capabilities;
* the ECF mapping is incomplete;
* or the capability legitimately spans domains.

---

27. No Forced ECF Coverage

The following shall be explicitly rejected:

"ECF cell is empty → create capability."

Instead:

ECF Cell
   │
   ├── populated
   ├── legitimately empty
   ├── indirectly represented
   └── requires further investigation

An empty cell is itself a valid analytical result.

36. Initial Research Finding

The first research pass already indicates that a recurring enterprise capability layer exists independently of industry-specific capabilities.

Independent sources identify recurring areas including finance, human capital, procurement, legal/compliance, risk, customer-related capabilities and technology, while industry-specific models add specialized capabilities above that common layer. (IF4IT⁠)

Business architecture evidence further supports the idea that capabilities are stable building blocks with a business-object focus and are distinct from the processes that realize them. (businessarchitectureguild.org⁠)

This supports proceeding with the investigation.

It does not yet justify finalizing the proposed capability names.

---

37. Important Negative Finding

The research also confirms that process frameworks are not suitable as direct capability catalogs.

APQC explicitly identifies its PCF as a process taxonomy, including process groups, processes, activities and tasks. (APQC⁠)

Therefore:

APQC process categories may be used to discover recurring enterprise work, but shall not be mechanically converted into DEA Business Capabilities.

This distinction is now an explicit research constraint.

41. Final Principle

The resulting catalog shall embody:

Discover, don’t invent. Normalize, don’t duplicate. Generalize, don’t prematurely specialize. Coordinate, don’t force-fit.

The first-order Business Capability Catalog is therefore an evidence-derived semantic asset, with the ECF providing its organizing coordinate system and specialization providing its controlled path into industry and enterprise-specific reality.

What I would do next

We should now actually execute CR-DEA-BC-02, rather than write another methodology document.

The first research pass should build the candidate universe from several independent evidence streams and then compare them. The important thing is that we already have a useful triangulation:

* Business Architecture Guild gives us strong evidence for capability semantics, business-object focus and the distinction between capability and value-stream/process perspectives. 
* SAP’s enterprise-architecture guidance independently reinforces capability as a stable, business-oriented “what”, distinct from process and implementation. 
* APQC gives us a very broad cross-industry view of recurring enterprise work, while explicitly identifying its material as a process taxonomy; this makes it excellent evidence for discovering underlying capabilities without allowing process categories to contaminate the capability model. 
* The current repository already reflects the methodological principles we approved, including the evidence ladder and ECF-as-coordinate approach. 

The next substantive artifact should therefore be the actual Candidate Universe v0.1, with each candidate backed by evidence, a provisional classification, enterprise-generality assessment, likely business-object focus, possible parent/child relationships, and an initial ECF hypothesis: while explicitly marking every item as candidate, not canonical.
